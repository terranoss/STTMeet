# Share one Gemini failover loop across all four call sites

Retry, model **failover**, **quota failure** detection and backoff were written out four times: `generate_meeting_minutes`, `generate_action_items`, `generate_meeting_recap` and `transcribe_audio_chunk`. The copies had already drifted — three back off by `attempt * 5` and one by `attempt * 4`, text generation pins `temperature=0.2` while the transcriber uses the configured `0.1`, and the two **quota failure** messages differ. None of the four had any test coverage, so a fix to the failure policy would have had to be made four times. We extracted `src/gemini_failover.py` as a sibling of `cancellation.py`, exposing `generate_with_failover` and a `FailoverCall` that carries what legitimately varies per site: the **failover chain**, the contents, the backoff step, the temperature, the final error message, and `prepare`/`cleanup` callbacks.

The transcription path's cloud-file lifecycle is the reason this is not simply "one more module": an upload must bracket one model's whole retry sweep and be deleted in a `finally`, while the text sites have no resource at all. Rather than push upload logic into the generic module, the transcriber supplies `prepare` and `cleanup` callbacks and the generic loop brackets them. Upload and delete stay in `gemini_transcriber.py`.

Divergence between the sites was deliberately preserved rather than unified. The backoff constants and the two **quota failure** messages are user-visible behaviour, and collapsing them would silently change transcription and text-generation resilience. They are now parameters, so they are at least visible in one place and changeable in one place.

`recap_generator.py` was kept rather than folded into `minutes_generator.py`: after the collapse it holds a prompt table and one call, which is an honest small module rather than a pass-through. Folding it in would have grown the minutes module to hold prompt tables for three unrelated documents.
